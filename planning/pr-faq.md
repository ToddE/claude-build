# Press Release

**inform9 Makes Collecting W-9 Forms Simple and Affordable for Small Businesses**

*Small businesses and sole proprietors can request, receive, and store W-9s from contractors and vendors for a low annual fee, with no accounting software required.*

RICHMOND, Va. -- December 29, 2026 -- inform9 today launched inform9.com, a service that lets small business owners request W-9 forms by email and keep every completed form in one secure place. The free plan includes one business and up to three payees. Paid plans are expected to cost between $20 and $36 per year.

Any business that pays contractors or vendors usually needs a W-9 from each one before filing year-end reports. Getting those forms is slow. Owners email a blank form, wait for a scan, photo, or fax, send reminders, and save each file in an inbox or a folder. At year end they search for the forms they never received. Accounting software such as Xero and QuickBooks includes W-9 requests, but plans start at about $28 per month. Owners who use other tools collect W-9s by hand. The free online options mostly promote a larger accounting product, and they are hard to use when you need one form.

inform9 does one job. The business owner signs in to inform9.com, adds a business (paid plans can add more), and creates a payee contact for each contractor or vendor. inform9 emails each payee a secure link. The payee fills out the W-9 online and signs it electronically, usually in a few minutes. If the payee does not respond, inform9 sends a reminder every seven days, up to three times, and the owner sees which payees have finished.

Payees can create a free account to save their information. When another business asks for a W-9, the payee clicks the link, signs in, reviews the saved information, and approves sending it with one click. inform9 never shares saved information without that approval. When a payee updates a W-9, the payee chooses which businesses receive the new version, and each business owner receives an email about the update.

Each business keeps its own signed copy of every W-9, including earlier versions. Owners view their payees, download each form, and export payee data as a file for QuickBooks, Xero, or other tools.

A payee with a saved W-9 can also send it to a business that has not asked for one. The recipient verifies their email address with a one-time code, then downloads the form without an account. Saving the form to an account that tracks payees and sends reminders takes a free account.

"Small business owners have a story about chasing a W-9 by email," said Todd Emerson, founder of inform9. "Filling out the form should take a payee a few minutes, and collecting it should take the owner almost no effort. We built inform9 to do that one job well, at a price any business can afford, whichever accounting software they use."

*[Hypothetical customer quote]* "I work with about a dozen freelancers each year, and I used to track their W-9s in an email folder," said Maria Alvarez, owner of a small marketing agency. "I sent the link, my designer filled it out that afternoon, and the form was in my account. I did not have to follow up."

To get started, visit inform9.com, create an account, add your business, and add your first payee contact. The free plan includes one business and up to three payees.

# FAQ

## External FAQ

**What does inform9 cost?**
The free plan includes one business and up to three payees. Paid plans are expected to cost between $20 and $36 per year. [Pricing tiers to be announced.] A payee who works with several of your businesses counts once.

**How does it work?**
You add your businesses to your account and create payee contacts, each linked to one or more of those businesses. inform9 emails each payee a secure link. The payee completes and signs the W-9 online, and the form is saved to the matching businesses in your account. You receive an email when a payee completes or updates a W-9. You can view your payees and download or manage their forms at any time.

**What does my payee need to do?**
Click the link in the email, fill out the W-9, and sign it electronically. Payees can create a free account to save their information for future requests. A payee with an account clicks the link, signs in, reviews the saved information, and approves sending it to the business. inform9 shares nothing without that approval.

**What if my payee does not respond?**
inform9 reminds the payee every seven days, up to three times. A payee gets at most one reminder email per day, even when several businesses are waiting on them. Reminders stop when the payee completes the form, declines, reports the wrong recipient, or chooses to stop reminders, when you cancel the request, or when the email bounces. You can resend a request up to a set number of times. On paid plans you can change the interval from 3 to 30 days and the number of reminders up to 6, for a business or for a single request.

**What happens when a payee updates a W-9?**
The payee chooses which businesses receive the updated form. Each of those businesses keeps the new version and the earlier ones, and you receive an email about the update.

**Can a payee send me a W-9 without my asking?**
Yes. You receive an email with a secure link. You verify your email address with a one-time code, and then you can download the W-9 without an account. The link expires within seven days, and each link allows three to five downloads in 24 hours. To keep the form, track the payee, and set reminders, create a free account. A saved payee counts toward the plan limit. Each email has an opt-out link that never expires. You can stop W-9s from one payee or from all payees, and you can report a message that reached you by mistake.

**Will inform9 send me marketing email?**
Only if you ask. When you create an account, an unchecked box lets you choose to receive updates and information about inform9 products and services. You can change this at any time in your account or with the unsubscribe link in any update email. Requests, reminders, receipts, and notices about your W-9s continue whatever you choose.

**Do I need accounting software to use inform9?**
No. inform9 works on its own and alongside any accounting tool.

### Product and Technical

**What edge cases need a defined answer?**
- A payee never responds, or the email lands in spam.
- The owner enters a wrong email address.
- A payee enters an incorrect taxpayer ID or name.
- A payee needs to submit an updated W-9, and some businesses should keep the older version.
- A payee is a foreign person who needs a different form. This is out of scope for the first release. [Confirm.]
- A payee-sent W-9 reaches the wrong person, or reaches someone who wants no such messages.
- A payee declines a request.

**What are the hard technical problems?**
- Storing taxpayer identification numbers securely.
- Email deliverability for requests, reminders, and one-time codes.
- Electronic signature that meets IRS conditions.
- A clear way for owners to see which payees have finished.
- Payee accounts that keep information for reuse across businesses. A payee must sign in and approve each request before inform9 shares saved information, so the email link leads to a sign-in or verification step first.
- Rules for which business sees which version of a W-9.
- Abuse controls for payee-sent W-9s: email verification of the recipient, send caps, opt-out, and a report option.

**What third-party dependencies exist?**
An email delivery service, cloud hosting and storage, and a payment processor for paid plans. [Select vendors.]

**How are platform settings managed?**
The inform9 administrator sets the payee-sent link expiry (up to seven days) and the daily download cap (three to five). Changes apply to links sent afterward. The first release may use environment variables. An administrator screen is the target, so changes do not need SQL queries or a redeploy. The platform logs each download so we can learn whether repeat downloads come from difficulty with the page or from other causes.

Payee count is the likely pricing lever, because it follows storage, email volume, and support load. A large free tier with later cross-sell of other products is under consideration. ### Risk and Constraints

**What are the top reasons inform9 could fail?**
1. A security incident. Storing taxpayer IDs for many businesses creates a high-value target and damages trust if anything goes wrong.
2. Low conversion to paid plans. Many sole proprietors may stay within three payees, and accounting vendors may add free W-9 tools.
3. Customer acquisition costs more than a $20 to $36 annual subscription earns.
4. Misuse of payee-sent W-9s for unwanted email, which harms deliverability and trust.

**Are there regulatory or legal issues?**
Yes. IRS conditions on electronic W-9 collection and signature, state privacy and breach notification laws, data retention rules, and email marketing rules (CAN-SPAM) all apply. Payees complete forms quickly, owners trust the security, and cost per customer stays low.
